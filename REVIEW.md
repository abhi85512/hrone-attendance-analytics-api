# REVIEW.md

Defects found in the starter `app/main.py` (helpers and endpoints):

| # | Where (function / line) | What is wrong | How you'd notice it (test, input, or symptom) | How you fixed it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `list_employees` (line 93) | Pagination offset calculation used `skip = page * page_size`. For page 1, this skipped `1 * page_size` items, missing the entire first page. | Calling `GET /employees?page=1&page_size=10` returned items starting from rank/index 11 rather than index 1. | Corrected offset formula to `skip = (page - 1) * page_size`. |
| 2 | `list_employees` (line 94) | Total count query `db.employees.count_documents({})` ignored the filter query `q`. | Calling `GET /employees?department=Engineering` returned filtered items but reported the unfiltered total count of all employees in the entire company. | Changed to `db.employees.count_documents(q)`. |
| 3 | `list_attendance` (lines 144–150) | In-memory sorting and pagination: called `list(db.attendance_logs.find(q))` loading all matching records into RAM, followed by Python `.sort()` and slice. | Severe latency and OOM danger on large collections (e.g. 100k+ logs); does not use MongoDB indexes. | Replaced with cursor-level `.sort([("date", -1), ("emp_code", 1)]).skip(skip).limit(page_size)` backed by compound index. |
| 4 | `punch_in` (line 102) | `datetime.fromtimestamp(body.punched_at / 1000)` created naive local datetime and did not truncate sub-second precision. | Stored timestamp had local machine offset and sub-second microseconds, violating business rule R1 (truncate to seconds) and causing inconsistent UTC comparisons. | Converted via `datetime.fromtimestamp(body.punched_at / 1000, tz=timezone.utc).replace(microsecond=0)`. |
| 5 | `punch_in` (line 101) | Direct dictionary lookup `emp["shift_start"]` without verifying if employee was found (`emp is None`). | Passing a non-existent `emp_code` raised an unhandled `TypeError` (HTTP 500 crash) instead of a clean HTTP 404 client error. | Added explicit check `if not emp: raise HTTPException(status_code=404, detail="Employee not found")`. |
| 6 | `compute_late_minutes` (lines 33–39) | Replaced `hour` and `minute` directly on UTC datetime without converting to the employee's business timezone (Asia/Kolkata / IST). | For a 09:00 IST shift (03:30 UTC), a punch-in at 09:05 IST (03:35 UTC) was compared against 09:00 UTC, producing wildly erroneous negative or invalid late durations. | Converted `punch_in` to IST (`ZoneInfo("Asia/Kolkata")`), built shift start on the attendance date in IST, and computed late duration in minutes against that boundary. |
| 7 | `compute_overtime` (lines 41–48) | Assumed shift end always falls on the same calendar day `date_str`. | For overnight shifts (e.g., 22:00 to 06:00), `shift_end` is on the next calendar day (`date + 1 day`). Comparing same-day timestamps generated massive negative or incorrect overtime. | Detected overnight shifts (`shift_end <= shift_start`) and added `timedelta(days=1)` to `shift_end_dt`. |
| 8 | `create_employee` (line 82) | Used `datetime.now()` creating a timezone-naive datetime for `created_at`. | Naive local datetimes in MongoDB cause ambiguous time offsets across servers and test runners. | Changed to timezone-aware `datetime.now(timezone.utc)`. |
| 9 | `punch_in` & `punch_out` (lines 98–140) | Check-then-act pattern without unique index or atomic operations allowed race conditions. | Concurrent identical punch-in requests both passed the read check and inserted duplicate logs for the same day. | Enforced compound unique index `(emp_code, date)` catching `DuplicateKeyError` (returning 409 Conflict), and used atomic `find_one_and_update(..., {"punch_out": None})` for punch-outs. |

### Considered and Decided NOT a Defect:
- **15-minute grace period threshold (`diff > 15`)**: Checked whether punch-in at exactly 15 minutes past shift start incurs late penalty. Rule R3 states punches within the 15-minute grace window are not penalized, but once exceeded by > 15 minutes, late minutes are counted from shift start. The `> 15` threshold accurately reflects R3.
- **Half-day penalty on 120+ minutes late**: Setting status to `HALF_DAY` when late minutes exceed 120 minutes matches the business requirement in R3 and is retained.
- **Date string storage format (`YYYY-MM-DD`)**: Keeping `date` as a canonical string in addition to BSON datetimes was verified against the data model specification and query indexes.
